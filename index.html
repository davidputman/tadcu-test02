Narrator The girls, for once, were precisely in the right field. So the trouble arrived by post.
Tadcu A parcel. For me. From Pontypool. Nerys’s ticket says three kilograms — Nerys counts kilograms the way you’d count trouble.
Bryn (ear up. Professional interest.)
Narrator Chariot first. Tea is for after.
Narrator The Post Office at Cwm Twp: half banking hall, half mild threat.
Tadcu Morning, Nerys! I’ve come for my parcel — the one with my name on it TWICE.
Nerys Morning, Tadcu. Signature first.
Tadcu I AM the signature, love.
Narrator The ticket said three kilograms. The scales said thirty. The truth, as ever, was stringier.
Tadcu I want it recorded that this parcel contains — ANOTHER PARCEL.
Nerys And I want it recorded that it contained another parcel when it ARRIVED.
Narrator The Boxing Day of parcels keeps on giving.
Nerys Keep going. Or I start charging you storage.
Narrator Down through cardboard, string and postal history — to one very small, very final box.
Tadcu (quiet) A thermos. New. From Eirlys — my sister, Pontypool. “Keep the fuel up.”
Narrator Tadcu, who has weathered every gate in the valley without a sniff, took something out of his eye. Nerys poured him a proper cup, and said nothing.
Nerys Two sugars? Or shall I just pass the tin over?
Narrator One thermos for the road — and ninety-nine ewes who could smell, at forty yards, that the bag held no sheep food whatsoever. They followed anyway.
Tadcu It’s TEA, girls. TEA. Were any of you raised in a barn?
Bryn (rides the parcel. Regal.)
Narrator And so the parcel kept its promise: it contained another parcel — right up until it contained tea.
